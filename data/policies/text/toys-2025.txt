 1. Overview
1.1. Introduction
Toys play a vital role in children’s lives, contributing to the development of cognitive, motor,
psychosocial, emotional, and linguistic skills. They are also instrumental in nurturing self-confidence
and creativity among children. Article - 31 of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child
(UNCRC) establishes children’s Right to Play, recognising it as a fundamental aspect of a child’s
development.
The global toy market is valued at USD 180 Billion in 2024 and is expected to reach USD 265 Billion
by 2028, growing at a 7% CAGR. Currently, India’s market share in this sector is less than 1%, valued
at USD 1.70 Billion . The major segments include activity and ride-on toys, educational toys, infant
and preschool toys, games and puzzles, plush toys, dolls, and electronic & remote-controlled toys.
Traditionally, India’s toy market was highly fragmented, import-orientated, and driven by locally
inspired cultural themes, handcrafted using indigenous materials. The growing global demand for
edutainment and advancements in material technology have transformed this industry.

With over 25% of India’s population under the age of 14, the country presents a substantial and
growing market for the toy industry. This growth is further driven by increasing urbanisation and a
rising preference for educational and interactive toys that cater to modern consumer preferences.
Tamil Nadu has a rich history of toy-making and traditional games that dates back to the Sangam
period. Traditional toys and games, such as Thanjavur Thalayatti Bommai, Pallanguzhi, Paramapadham,
and Dayakattai, reflect the State’s culture, heritage, and craftsmanship. These toys, often handcrafted
from natural materials like wood, clay, palm leaves, and cloth, showcase intricate designs, vibrant
colours, and a cultural uniqueness that is inherent to the region. Traditional toys hold significant
potential for the economic empowerment of local artisans. Given the promising growth of the toy
industry globally, alongside India’s aspirations to capture a larger share of the global market, this
sector presents an attractive opportunity for Tamil Nadu’s industrial ecosystem.
The Tamil Nadu Toy Manufacturing Policy aims to harness the State’s strong manufacturing ecosystem
to drive innovation in toy design, development, and production while strengthening traditional toy
clusters.

1.2. Key Trends Driving Manufacturing & Innovation in the
     Toy Industry
i. Toy Licensing Agreements
Toy manufacturers use the intellectual property of popular brands and characters to create and
market toys. Licensing agreements with popular franchises will continue to be a key strategy for toy
manufacturers.
ii. Emphasis on STEM Education
With a growing emphasis on STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) education,
toy manufacturers are developing products that encourage children to explore these areas, helping
them develop problem-solving skills, critical thinking, and creativity.
iii. Greater Emphasis on Safety and Quality
The global regulatory landscape for toys involve stringent safety and material-related regulations,
such as the American Society for Testing and Materials (F963-17) and the European Toy Safety
Directive (2009/48/EC), to safeguard children. In India, the Toys (Quality Control) Order of February
2020 mandates the compulsory use of the Standard Mark and certification by the Bureau of Indian
Standards (BIS).
1
iv. Inclusive Toy Design
Toys for children with special needs require new designs and manufacturing techniques to foster
cognitive, sensory, and motor development.
v. Eco-Friendly Toys
Growing environmental consciousness among consumers is driving demand for eco-friendly toys
made from sustainable materials. Manufacturers are integrating recycled materials and adopting
waste-reduction measures to meet these expectations.




1.3. Synergies for Toy Manufacturing in Tamil Nadu
The manufacturing capabilities of Tamil Nadu, spanning diverse sectors such as auto components,
electronics, textiles, and engineering, offer a strong foundation for the toy industry. The State has the
potential to attract local and global toy makers to establish high-end toy manufacturing, including
electromechanical toys.

Further, the established electronics sector in the State provides advanced infrastructure essential for
high-end toys, particularly interactive toys. The robust injection moulding and die-casting capabilities
are well-suited for producing durable and intricate toy components.

Tamil Nadu’s focus on sustainable practices aligns well with eco-friendly toy manufacturing, meeting
the needs of a conscious consumer base. As a leader in textiles, Tamil Nadu can support the plush and
fabric toy market with high-quality and safe materials. Proximity to textile hub fosters opportunities
for collaboration and innovation in textile-based toys.

This synergy makes Tamil Nadu an attractive hub for manufacturing world-class toys that appeal to
both domestic and global markets.

    2. Objectives
2.1. Vision
To position Tamil Nadu as a global hub for design, development, and manufacturing of new-age toys
and to champion the global recognition of Tamil Nadu’s traditional toy heritage.

2.2. Targets
i. Attract at least 10 anchor players in the toy manufacturing industry.

ii. Establish a dedicated Toy Manufacturing Park in the State with a supporting ecosystem and
    infrastructure.

iii. Attract leading design entities to set up their creative studios in the State.

iv. Identify and promote traditional toy clusters in Tamil Nadu to make them globally competitive.

2.3. Scope
The policy aims to attract investments from reputable domestic and international players in toy design
and manufacturing . Additionally, the policy supports ancillary and upstream entities involved in the
development of raw materials (polymers/recycled textiles), components, and design. Furthermore, the
policy outlines necessary market interventions to support traditional and artisanal toy manufacturing
in the State.

The following sub-segments in toy manufacturing will be prioritised under the
policy:

2.3.1. Ancillary Industries
Ancillary industries deriving at least 50% of their annual turnover from sales to toy manufacturing
units shall be covered under this policy.

 3. Incentives for Toy Manufacturing
Projects that are ‘Mega’ and ‘Ultra-Mega’ in “A” Category Districts (Rs. 500 Crore and above) and
‘Large’ or above in “B” and “C” Category Districts (Rs. 300 Crore and above) are eligible for the
“Structured Package of Assistance” as per the Tamil Nadu Industrial Policy (TNIP) 2021. The toy
manufacturing industry shall be treated as a sunrise sector under TNIP 2021 and shall be eligible for
“Special Incentives for Sunrise Sector,” which includes Investment Promotion Subsidy, Land Cost
Subsidy, Stamp Duty Incentive, Enhanced Incentive for Quality Certification, Enhanced Incentive for
Intellectual Property Creation, Interest Subvention, and Electricity Tax Exemption.
A company can choose to avail incentives under the “Special Package of Incentives for Toy
Manufacturing” under this policy or the “Special Incentives for Sunrise Sector” under TNIP 2021,
subject to satisfying the eligibility criteria of the applicable policy.

3.1. Special Package of Incentives for Toy Manufacturing
Eligible Units
To attract new investments in this niche sector and encourage domestic players to diversify into
this segment, new/expansion projects with investments greater than Rs. 50 Crore and minimum
employment of 50 jobs shall be eligible for the “Special Package of Incentives for Toy Manufacturing”
under this policy. The minimum investment and employment commitments must be fulfilled within
the Standard Investment Period of 3 years.

3.1.1. Fixed Capital Subsidy
Eligible units can avail Fixed Capital Subsidy as provided in the table below:

 District Category                 Fixed Capital Subsidy (% of EFA/Disbursal Period)

     ‘A’ District                                      7% / 5 years

     ‘B’ District                                     10% / 10 years

     ‘C’ District                                     12% / 10 years

The Fixed Capital Subsidy shall be disbursed in equal annual instalments over the incentive disbursal
period upon fulfilment of investment and employment commitments and commencement of
operations from the plant.

3.1.2. Traceability Incentive
To support the establishment of an end-to-end system that enhances traceability and quality, 50%
of the expenditure incurred on implementing traceability systems (hardware & software) shall be
refunded for a period of three years from the commencement of operations from the plant, subject to
a ceiling of Rs. 25 Lakh.

3.1.3. Training Subsidy
Skilling support shall be provided in the form of a Training Subsidy of Rs. 4,000 per worker per month
for 6 months for residents of Tamil Nadu. For women and transgender employees, individuals with
benchmark disabilities, and individuals from SC/ST communities, the training subsidy shall be Rs.
6,000 per worker per month for 6 months.

3.1.4. Designer Training Subsidy
A training subsidy of Rs. 10,000 per month per designer shall be offered for 6 months for residents of
Tamil Nadu, subject to a maximum of 50 employees.
                                                                                                     4
3.1.5. Enhanced Incentive for Quality Certification
Projects obtaining certifications like BIS/ISO, or any other national or international certification shall
receive a subsidy of 50% of the total cost incurred for obtaining the certification, as certified by a
Chartered Accountant, limited to Rs. 1 Crore for the period of investment.

3.1.6. Enhanced Intellectual Property Creation Incentive
The Government will reimburse 50% of the expenditure incurred by the project, subject to a maximum
of Rs. 1 Crore during the period of investment for a patent, copyright, trademark, and Geographical
Indication (GI) registration.

The following subsidies (3.1.7 To 3.1.9) shall be applicable, subject to meeting the eligibility criteria as
per TNIP 2021.

3.1.7. Land Cost Subsidy
For eligible projects in SIPCOT, land allotment will be made at a 10% concessional rate in “A” & “B”
districts and at a 50% concessional rate in “C” districts for land up to 20% of EFA. For private land in
“C” districts, a 50% subsidy will be offered on the cost of land as per guideline value up to an extent
of 50 acres, subject to land cost not exceeding 20% of EFA and a cap of Rs. 2 Crore, provided that at
least 70% of the land is used for manufacturing operations. If the investor chooses to avail the land
cost subsidy, the land will be excluded from Eligible Fixed Assets for the purpose of the Investment
Promotion Subsidy.

3.1.8. Stamp Duty Incentive
A 100% stamp duty exemption will be given for the lease or purchase of land/shed/buildings meant
for industrial use on land obtained from SIPCOT. For private lands, a stamp duty concession will be
provided as a 100% back-end subsidy for up to 50 acres upon fulfilment of investment and employment
commitments.

3.1.9. Electricity Tax Exemption
Eligible units will be granted an electricity tax exemption for a period of 5 years on power purchased
from the Tamil Nadu Power Distribution Corporation Limited (TNPDCL) or generated and consumed
from captive sources.

3.2. Incentives for MSME units
Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprise units—as defined under the MSME Policy, 2021 - which are not
eligible for the incentives listed under Section 3.1 – Special Package of Incentives for Toy Manufacturing
may avail the following incentives as per the Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSME) Policy
2021, subject to meeting the eligibility criteria:

3.2.1. Capital Subsidy
a. Small and Medium Enterprises
Eligible units setting up in industrially backward blocks (as notified by Government) and all industrial
estates promoted by the Government and Government agencies like SIPCOT & TANSIDCO, except
those located in industrial estates within a radius of 50 km from Chennai city centre, can avail a
capital subsidy equivalent to 25% of plant & machinery value, with a maximum of up to Rs. 1.50 Crore
in three instalments.
b. Micro Enterprises
Eligible units located anywhere in the State can avail of a capital subsidy equivalent to 25% of plant
& machinery value, with a maximum of up to Rs. 25 Lakh.

3.2.2. Additional Capital Subsidy for Women, SC/ST, Differently Abled
       & Transgender Entrepreneurs
An additional capital subsidy of 5% on the value of eligible plant and machinery, subject to a maximum
of Rs. 5 Lakh, to enterprises set up by Women/Scheduled Caste/Scheduled Tribe/Differently abled/
Transgender entrepreneurs. The subsidy is applicable to all eligible micro enterprises established
anywhere in the State and Small & Medium enterprises being set up in industrially backward blocks
and all industrial estates promoted by the Government and Government agencies like SIPCOT &
TANSIDCO, except those located in industrial estates within the radius of 50 km from Chennai city
centre.
3.2.3. Subsidy for Adoption of Sustainable Technologies
An additional capital subsidy of 25% on the value of eligible plant and machinery/equipment (for
items not covered under capital subsidy) installed to promote cleaner and environment-friendly
technologies, subject to a maximum of Rs. 10 Lakh. This subsidy shall be applicable to all new
Micro manufacturing enterprises set up anywhere in Tamil Nadu and all new Small and Medium
manufacturing enterprises set up in declared backward blocks and industrial estates, excluding those
located in industrial estates within the radius of 50 km from Chennai city centre.

3.2.4. Reimbursement of Stamp Duty and Registration Charges
Reimbursement of stamp duty and registration charges for purchase of land meant for industrial use,
at the rate of 50% of the actual value, will be extended to eligible Micro and Small manufacturing
enterprises set up in declared backward blocks. This incentive is admissible to eligible enterprises on
the land area up to five times the plinth area of the factory building constructed.

3.2.5. Low Tension Power Tariff Subsidy
New Micro-enterprises or existing enterprises undergoing expansion and diversification using Low
Tension Power Supply (Tariff III B) can avail an amount equivalent to 20% of power consumption
charges paid to TANGEDCO for the first 36 months from the date of commencement of production or
the date of receiving the power connection, whichever is later.

3.2.6. Reimbursement of Energy Audit and Implementation Charges
Eligible units can avail a 75% subsidy on the cost of taking up an energy audit, subject to a maximum
of Rs. 1 Lakh per energy audit, and 50% of the cost of eligible components, subject to a maximum of
Rs. 10 Lakh for implementing the recommendations of the energy audit.

3.2.7. Payroll Subsidy
Reimbursement of employers’ contribution to the EPF for the first three years from the date of
commencement of commercial production shall be provided if the enterprise employs greater than
20 persons or employs 25% of its workforce as differently abled individuals, subject to a ceiling of Rs.
24,000 per person per annum.

The subsidy shall be applicable to all Micro enterprises and all new SMEs employing more than 25%
of their workforce as differently abled, setting up anywhere in the State. It shall also be applicable
to Small and Medium Enterprises establishing units in industrially backward blocks and industrial
estates promoted by the Government and its agencies like SIPCOT and TANSIDCO, except those
located in industrial estates within the radius of 50 km from Chennai city centre.

3.2.8. Back-Ended Interest Subsidy
Eligible units will be supported with a 5% back-ended interest subsidy for a maximum of 5 years for
term loans taken up to Rs. 2 Crore with Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises
(CGTMSE) coverage or up to Rs. 5 Crore taken up for technology upgradation/modernisation, subject
to a maximum subsidy of Rs. 20 Lakh and Rs. 25 Lakh, respectively.

3.2.9. Credit-linked Subsidies
Eligible MSME units can avail support under existing credit-linked subsidy schemes such as the New
Entrepreneur cum Enterprise Development Scheme (NEEDS) for first-generation entrepreneurs,
the Annal Ambedkar Business Champions Scheme (AABCS) for entrepreneurs from the SC/ST
community, the Kalaignar Kaivinai Thittam (KKT) for those involved in artisanal trades including doll/

3.2.10. Incentives for Quality Certification
Eligible units shall be supported with 100% reimbursement of certification and consulting charges
subject to a maximum of Rs. 2 Lakh per unit for National certifications and Rs. 10 Lakh per unit for

A unit with a valid Udyam Registration can obtain multiple certifications within the given financial
limit. A unit can also claim this amount for new certifications on scaling up to the next category.

3.2.11. Incentives for Intellectual Property Creation
a. Patent Registration
Eligible entities can avail a 75% subsidy on the cost of filing the application for patent registration,
including the cost of the first-time maintenance fee, subject to a maximum of Rs. 3 Lakh per patent
registered.
b. For Trademark Registration and Geographical Indication
Eligible entities can avail a 50% subsidy on the cost of filing an application for trademark registration,
including the cost of the first-time maintenance fee, subject to a maximum of Rs. 25,000 per trademark
or Geographical Indication (GI).

3.2.12. Innovation Voucher Program, TANFUND and Listing Support in
SME Exchange
Through the innovation voucher program, grants between Rs. 2 - 5 Lakh shall be provided to support
scaling up of innovative ideas towards prototyping and commercialisation, accordingly. Eligible
startups shall be supported with funding and access to networking/mentor support through the
TANFUND promoted by Startup TN. One-time assistance of 75% of expenditure incurred, subject to
a cap of Rs. 30 Lakh for listing in the SME exchange, shall be provided.

3.2.13. Marketing Support
Reverse Buyer-Seller Meets (RBSM), Vendor Development Programs, and B2B meetings with Public
Sector Undertakings (PSUs) and large industries shall be facilitated on a need basis between MSMEs
and large anchor buyers from India and abroad.
•   As per the MSME policy 2021, MSME associations in the State shall be supported with
    reimbursement of 50% of the hall rent for conducting exhibitions in India and 50% of the rent paid
    towards stalls or space charges, subject to a maximum of Rs. 7.5 Lakh for domestic events and up
    to Rs. 15 Lakh for international events.

•   As per the FaMeTN Sponsorship Policy, individual MSME units can claim reimbursements for
    expenditures incurred towards setting up stalls, participating in seminars/conferences, exhibitions,
    and buyer-seller meets in domestic and international events, subject to meeting eligibility criteria.

5. Support for Toy Manufacturing for Children with
   Special Needs
Recognising the importance of catering to the special needs of differently abled children, the State
shall promote the production of specialised toys designed to aid in cognitive, motor, and sensory
development. By encouraging manufacturers to integrate universal design principles, these toys can
offer a blend of functionality and engagement that meets specific developmental needs.

This focused approach could further position Tamil Nadu as a leader in inclusive toy manufacturing,
fostering innovation and socially conscious entrepreneurship. The State shall encourage local
production of toys that adhere to global safety and accessibility standards, making Tamil Nadu a hub
for inclusive toy manufacturing.

The State shall support such manufacturers with custom incentive packages and partnerships with
organisations specialising in care of differently abled individuals.


6. Promotion of Creative Design Studios
The toy industry’s trend-driven nature demands advanced design and R&D to meet consumer
preferences for educational, STEM-based, and eco-friendly toys. Establishing design studios in the
State shall enable local toy manufacturers to become globally competitive, support SMEs, and create
high-end jobs in STEM, engineering, and the arts, strengthening the State’s creative landscape.

Creative design studios undertaking toy design or R&D related to toys, with clearly demarcated
facilities in the State, shall be provided a 30% payroll subsidy for 12 months, subject to a cap of
Rs. 10,000 per month per employee for new hires employed during the policy period who are residents
of Tamil Nadu.

 7. Artisanal Toy Ecosystem in Tamil Nadu
The traditional toys from Tamil Nadu are not only a testament to the State’s rich cultural heritage but
also offer significant potential for the economic empowerment of local artisans.

The clay toys of Tamil Nadu are famed for their artistic charm, vibrant colours, and wide range of
toys and decorative items. The Vilacheri toy cluster in Madurai holds rich cultural significance, with
artisans passing down their trade and craft through generations. Prominent centres in contemporary
times include Chennai, Kanchipuram, and parts of the Ranipet district.

Another toy-making tradition in the State is the wooden lacquerware toys crafted in the villages
of Ambasamudram taluk in the Tirunelveli district. These toys include miniature birds, musical
instruments, kids’ walkers, cradle sticks, and miniature kitchen sets.

Inspired by the traditional dance forms, the Thanjavur Thalayatti Bommai (dancing doll) is recognised
under the Geographical Indication (GI) tag for its unique design and cultural significance. These dolls
have become one of the iconic faces of the State, serving as attractive souvenirs.

The Tamil Nadu Handicrafts Development Corporation, Poompuhar, in collaboration with the
Development Commissioner for Handicrafts, Government of India, has developed and continues to
implement several programs to support the revival of traditional art forms.

7.2. Artisan Cluster Development Initiatives
The State shall support traditional artisans and manufacturers engaged in creating toys and games by
enhancing the quality and appeal of their products, encouraging product innovation, and promoting
diversification.

Additionally, market research studies shall be undertaken to assess the capacity of toy manufacturing
MSMEs and artisans in Tamil Nadu to cater to the global demand and to explore strategies to elevate
the global recognition of traditional artisanal toy products from Tamil Nadu.

The State, through IC&DIC, shall identify clusters for the following focused development initiatives:

7.2.1. Infrastructure Development
Under the Micro Cluster Development Program (MCDP), the State is developing a toy cluster in
Madurai. The State shall undertake a detailed mapping of the infrastructural requirements, Common
Facility centres (CFCs), tooling, and storage needs of similar toy clusters to support artisanal and
traditional toy manufacturers across the State.
•   Artisanal toy manufacturers currently face significant disruptions in wood preparation activities,
    such as cutting, chiselling, smoothening, and colouring, during the monsoon season due to
    inadequate infrastructure. The CFCs shall address this gap by providing well-equipped workspaces,
    ensuring continuity of production irrespective of seasonal constraints.

•   The CFCs shall also include a workshop area serving as a foundational space for all equipment, a
    designated work floor area, and the necessary tooling and polishing facilities.

•   Further, an inventory area with adequate storage space for raw materials, tools, and finished
    goods shall also be facilitated.

•   As part of the CFCs, a Design Development Centre with a Prototype Tool-Making Unit shall be
    established to develop various tools such as wood turning lathe machines, hand saws, chisels, and
    gauges, tailored to localised requirements and suited to address intricate design requirements.

7.2.2. Skilling Initiatives
Skill upgradation programs shall be developed in partnership with leading design schools and
institutions and implemented through the Tamil Nadu Skill Development Corporation (TNSDC), with
the support of community organisations and craft associations.

Targeted capacity-building initiatives will be undertaken through the Entrepreneurship Development
and Innovation Institute (EDII) to upskill artisans for toy commercialisation, covering scalability,
market demand, logistics, financial literacy, profitability, international standards, safety, sustainability,
and packaging.

Specific modules shall be developed to encourage artisans to use natural, non-toxic, and environment-
friendly materials in toy manufacturing. Artisans shall also be trained in sustainable practices, allowing
them to meet the growing global demand for eco-conscious products.

7.2.3. Financial Assistance for GI Tag
To promote the acquisition of GI tags for traditional toys in the State, financial assistance towards
legal fees, capped at Rs. 3 Lakh per toy category, shall be provided to clusters undertaking efforts for
approval.
                                                                                                          12
7.2.4. Market Access
The State shall facilitate market access by strengthening forward linkages in both traditional and
modern channels. In addition, the artisans shall be assisted in listing their products on major
e-commerce platforms and participating in local and international trade fairs, exhibitions, and
government-backed marketing initiatives. Under district-level MSME support initiatives, activities
such as buyer-seller meets, e-commerce onboarding workshops, financial and export workshops,
and quality control workshops shall be undertaken.

7.2.5. Experience Tourism Development
Toy clusters will be developed with facilities for heritage or toy enthusiasts to experience traditional
toy-making firsthand. Synergies will be identified to link these clusters with key tourism destinations
and corridors identified under the Tamil Nadu Tourism Policy 2023. To facilitate experience tourism
within the identified clusters, the State shall develop tourism amenities & toy museum centres.

7.3. Design Innovation and Technology Integration
The State shall encourage collaborations between artisans, design institutes, and technology
partners to introduce fresh, innovative designs that complement traditional techniques. Technology
integration, such as 3D prototyping and digital marketing, shall also be promoted to help artisans
expand their reach and enhance production efficiency. Digital design tools shall be developed to
support artisans in adopting the latest sustainable designs without the need for prototyping.

8. Policy Implementation
The incentive packages under this policy will be sanctioned to eligible industries by the Industries,
Investment Promotion, and Commerce Department based on the recommendation from Guidance
Tamil Nadu to the Government/Inter-Departmental Committee. SIPCOT will serve as the nodal agency
for incentive disbursal. The existing institutional mechanism for disbursal of investment-related
incentives to industries and other implementation guidelines (as per TNIP 2021 or the prevailing
industrial policy) will be applicable to eligible industries under this policy.

The incentives for the MSME sector will be sanctioned by the Commissioner of Industries and
Commerce. Single-window facilitation for the MSME sector will be undertaken by the Commissioner
of Industries and Commerce & District Industries Centre.

The initiatives discussed under Section 7, Artisanal toys ecosystem in Tamil Nadu, shall be implemented
by the MSME Department.

 9. Policy Period
The policy will be valid for a period of 5 years from the date of notification or until a new policy is
announced.
